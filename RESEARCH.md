# Which frontier can earn its first dollar?

Decision memo, November 24, 2026. Evidence comes from public primary sources. Rankings, prices and costs below are hypotheses. No buyer interviews, orders or revenue were available for this assessment.

## Decision

**Fastest plausible first profit.** Sell a specialist a fixed-price verification job that replaces work they already perform. Get one representative input and agree on an output that the buyer can check. This can produce contribution on the first order even when the whole product has yet to recover its build cost. Custom integration and founder selling time can consume the margin.

**Largest speculative distribution opportunity.** Sell a trusted, machine-readable result repeatedly to other software. It can expand without a human negotiating each lookup. The hard part is finding a result that people already purchase frequently enough to support it. A payment protocol alone gives little evidence of that demand.

**Build choice.** Apply verification to catastrophe finance. A specialty insurance broker can use Radius Clause to see which historical storms change their payout if a client signs the second wording. It fits the interest in finance and event settlement, provides a visually inspectable result, and starts with public storm observations. The first proposed sale is a human-reviewed, one-site comparison. An archive or integration for broker software is a possible expansion after repeat purchases.

## Why the instrument is real

Jamaica's 2024 CAR 136 bond offered $150 million of protection against named storms. Hurricane Beryl caused losses without activating that bond. In 2025 Hurricane Melissa triggered the full $150 million. The real bond used a pressure grid, a specified reporting process and contract-specific calculation dates. Its outcome demonstrates why wording matters. Radius Clause uses a different, explicitly hypothetical contract. [Jamaican government on Beryl](https://jis.gov.jm/update-on-the-disaster-risk-financing-instruments-from-the-minister-of-finance-and-the-public-service/) · [CAR 136 final terms](https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/2744f96ba4d88e82921b654480991fe4-0340022024/original/ft2024-XS2806459314.pdf) · [World Bank on Melissa](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2025/11/07/hurricane-melissa-triggers-100-payout-of-150-million-world-bank-catastrophe-bond-for-jamaica)

Swiss Re describes a worked hotel example using the highest qualifying wind recorded at track points within a circle. Descartes describes commercial policies using either storm proximity and intensity or modeled wind at a location. Brokers and clients already co-design triggers. Carriers already provide historical comparisons, so a generic hurricane map has little commercial leverage. The proposed wedge is an independent side-by-side comparison a broker can hand to a client, with the observation behind each disputed outcome. A broker who already receives an adequate comparison free from their carrier has little reason to switch. [Swiss Re example](https://corporatesolutions.swissre.com/insights/knowledge/what-is-the-most-popular-parametric-solution-cat-in-a-box-explained.html) · [Descartes contract design](https://descartesunderwriting.com/solutions/parametric-hurricane-insurance-na) · [Descartes on historical comparisons](https://descartesunderwriting.com/faq)

NOAA supplies retrospective best tracks with six-hourly and additional observations such as landfalls. The visualization connects points, and the calculation uses only individual archived observations. Revisions can change a historical replay. Wind at the center fix refers to the maximum wind anywhere in the storm. An exposed hotel can experience different wind, surge, interruption and damage. The loss overlay scores the user's supplied history and leaves absent observations unknown. [NOAA data](https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/data/) · [HURDAT2 specification](https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/data/hurdat/hurdat2-format-nov2019.pdf)

## The first $500 experiment

Offer a specialty broker one named site, two compatible draft wind/radius schedules, a historical storm comparison, and a short review call. The broker approves the rule translation, then receives a source-linked report identifying changes, near misses, uncovered losses on supplied records and exclusions. Atypical clauses, local wind footprints and pressure-grid agreements require separate work. The price is an experiment. The public workbench is free.

| Illustrative one-order economics | Assumption | Cost |
|---|---|---:|
| Founder analysis, QA and delivery | 3 hours at $60/hour | $180 |
| Sales time including unsuccessful leads | 2 hours attributed to each paid order at $60/hour | $120 |
| Support and administration | 1 hour at $60/hour | $60 |
| Payment fees and allocated delivery overhead | Allowance | $20 |
| **Contribution from a $500 order** | **$500 less $380** | **$120** |

On an illustrative $2,000 startup basis (30 build hours at $60 plus $200 overhead), 17 similar orders would cover the startup cost before tax. Two extra founder hours per order erase the contribution. Broker access, actual sales effort, data rights, conversion, insurance-related overhead and support could materially change these numbers. This calculation models feasibility and makes no revenue forecast.

Talk to five specialty brokers about their last comparable renewal. Ask to see two redacted schedules, their existing comparison and the time it took. The gate for further investment is two independent shops providing compatible inputs and one paying for a report. Track the analyst hours and whether the buyer returns at the next renewal. If every deal needs a new hazard model, free incumbent reports suffice, or five qualified prospects decline the paid test, revisit the offer before building a hosted API. If the work repeats, add portfolio comparisons and a versioned evidence endpoint first. Automated payments become relevant when an external client repeatedly buys it.
